## Phase 8 (ads): done
Sponsored placement, paid per click out of the seller's own earnings.
- **Sellers** (studio, Ads): campaigns with a name, the pieces to promote, a most-they-will-pay bid, a daily budget and an optional total budget; optional targeting by category and by search words (empty = the categories each piece belongs to, and searches that match it). Pause, resume, end. Numbers: views, clicks, click rate, spend, today against the daily budget, 14 days. Each campaign says in plain words why it is not running (paused, budget reached, stopped by staff, not enough earnings).
- **Shoppers** see up to two pieces marked "Sponsored" in category and search results, in fixed slots (after the 2nd and 7th result); the organic order is otherwise untouched and a sponsored piece is not shown twice.
- **The auction:** highest bid wins, one ad per seller per page, the winner pays one cent over the next bid down (never below the platform minimum, never above its own bid). Only pieces that are live, in stock, outside restricted categories, from an active seller who has earnings to pay for the click, within both budgets, and matching the page qualify.
- **Money:** a charged click is one balanced ledger entry (seller payable down, new `ad_revenue` account up). Clicks use a signed, single-use token that carries the price; tampered, replayed or expired tokens do nothing; the seller's own team and a repeat click from the same address within 30 minutes are free and not counted. Finance shows ad revenue; the whole ledger still sums to zero.
- **Staff** (marketing, Ads): see every campaign, stop one that breaks the rules with a reason (the seller sees it and cannot restart it) and release it later. Admin settings control the minimum bid and the number of slots; the `ads` feature flag switches it all off.
Verified: API **107 tests** (9 new: validation and ownership, restricted and unpublished pieces refused, eligibility, search and category matching, one-per-seller, slot setting, second-price pricing, single-use and tampered tokens, balanced ledger, repeat and own-team clicks, daily budget stop, empty balance, staff stop and release, finance ad revenue, feature flag). `apps/e2e/ads-flow.mjs`: **18 checks** in a real browser (two competing sellers, marked cards, order and slot, search ads, a $0.61 charged click against a 60-cent runner-up, a repeat click filtered, pause, staff stop with the seller seeing the reason, release). Earlier suites pass in order on a fresh seed: 23 visual screens, buyer (25), studio (38), consoles (44), engagement (27).
Not done: charging by view, conversion tracking and cost per sale, product-level quality scoring (the auction is bid only), ad creative beyond the product card, top-up wallets and invoices for ad spend (spend comes out of earnings), real-time fraud scoring beyond single-use tokens, the repeat-address rule and the own-team rule, scheduling campaigns by date.

## Phase 10 (hardening): done
- **Dependencies:** sharp 0.35.5 and zod 4.6.7 (the two high-severity findings); `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 0.
- **Security sweep (`test/security.test.ts`):** every registered route is listed automatically and checked for sign-in required, the right window and role, the origin (CSRF) check on writes, and no 5xx under malformed input. The auth guard is now deny-by-default (a new route is closed until it is listed as open). The sweep found one real gap: a NUL character in a URL or body became a database error (500); it is now a 400 everywhere.
- **Production safety:** the server refuses to start in production with development defaults (database URL, non-https origins, short or placeholder secrets, no SMTP, mock payments) and warns on the rest. Graceful shutdown on SIGTERM/SIGINT (stops accepting, drains, stops the job timer, closes the pool, exit 0). `/healthz` (alive) and `/readyz` (database reachable). Structured JSON logs with a request id, secrets and tokens redacted, ids in paths collapsed.
- **Privacy:** a signed-in person can download all their data and delete their account (password required; refused for staff, makers and while an order is on its way). Sales records stay for the books with no name, email or address left on them.
- **Performance (20,000 products, 343 shops, `npm run seed:load`):** search text and trigram indexes (migration 012), the count query no longer joins variants unless a price or shipping filter needs it, facets and rows run in parallel, listings and home share answers for 10 s (`LIST_CACHE_MS`). Category page 1751 → 154 ms, handmade filter 368 → 82 ms, product page 184 → 79 ms. A broad one-word search ("mug", thousands of matches) is still about 970 ms at 20 concurrent requests.
- **A real bug found by stress testing:** 40 buyers checking out at once hung the server. Checkout held a transaction connection while asking the pool for a second one for settings, so a full pool deadlocked. Fixed by passing the transaction's own connection (settings, seller balance); the pool is now 20 (`DB_POOL_MAX`), waits at most 10 s for a connection, and every statement has a 30 s limit, lock waits 15 s, idle transactions 30 s. Now: 40 buyers for 10 units gives exactly 10 orders and 30 refusals, stock never negative, and 20 identical payment webhooks at once apply once.
- **Backups (`ops/backup.sh`, `ops/restore-verify.sh`):** `pg_dump` custom format plus uploads, a manifest of row counts and the ledger sum, a checksum and 14-day pruning. The restore script loads a backup into a scratch database and checks every count, that the ledger sums to zero and that all migrations are present. Run for real on the dev database (12 orders) and the 20,000-product database: both verified; the large one took 0.8 s to back up (2.6 MB) and 2 s to restore.
- **Accessibility (`apps/e2e/a11y.mjs`):** axe-core (WCAG 2.1 A/AA and best practice) on 34 screens, light and dark, 68 scans; 320 px reflow; Tab order and visible focus. Fixed: no main landmark on the shop, sign-in and onboarding screens, an unnamed progress bar, a bad ARIA attribute on the photo strip, and the search box had no visible keyboard focus. No sideways scrolling at 320 px on any screen.
Verified: API **141 tests** in 14 files (34 new). The 23 visual tests all pass (largest difference 1.18%, limit 1.5%), run on a fresh seed and again after the web changes; all six browser flows pass; type checks (api, web), token lint and `npm audit --omit=dev` are clean.
**Open accessibility findings, for your decision (not changed, because they come from the approved mockups):** (1) The terracotta tag on a pale peach background (`#a9532d` on `#f3e4d9`) is 4.26:1; WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for small text. (2) Product cards use an `h4` straight after the page heading; changing the tag level risks the card styling, so I left it. It is a "best practice" notice, not an AA failure.
**Not verified:** behaviour under real internet traffic or on production-sized hardware (the load numbers are one machine, one database, the API and the load tool sharing the CPU); backups and restore on a managed database service, and point-in-time recovery (nothing here archives the write-ahead log, so the recovery point is the last backup: with a nightly job, up to 24 hours of orders); a screen-reader session (axe finds about a third of real issues; a person with a screen reader has not been through it); keyboard use beyond six screens; contrast on images and on pages not in the scan; the account-deletion wording by a lawyer (this is a mechanism, not legal advice about Canadian privacy law); penetration testing by a person.
